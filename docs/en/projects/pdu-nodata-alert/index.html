A power alert that was not a power problem

A rack power distribution unit stopped reporting to monitoring. The trigger said no data, and everyone read that as no power.

Problem

A high severity alert fired on a rack PDU. The natural reading of a power device going silent is that the power is gone, which would mean everything on that strip was down.

Approach

I checked what the trigger actually measures. A no data trigger fires when values stop arriving, which is a reporting problem and says nothing directly about the electrical state. I also looked at the raw values and found impossible readings on the phase items, which pointed at a template mismatch rather than a hardware fault.

Solution

The likely causes were narrowed to a hung SNMP agent, bulk request handling, poller backlog or items going unsupported, combined with a monitoring template that did not match the actual PDU model. I documented both the diagnosis and a plain language explanation of what a rack PDU is and does, so the next person on call does not start from zero.

Result

The alert was correctly classified as a monitoring fault, not an outage, and the template mismatch became a fixable ticket instead of a mystery.

What I learned

  • Know the difference between a threshold trigger and a no data trigger before you act on one.
  • Impossible raw values, like a negative reading on a physical measurement, almost always mean a wrong template or wrong OIDs.
